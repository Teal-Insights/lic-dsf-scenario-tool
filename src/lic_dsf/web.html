LIC-DSF Scenario Analysis Tool

Explore alternative macroeconomic assumptions, compare debt outcomes and prepare a briefing.

Early-stage preview. This version supports one World Bank LIC-DSF template and a limited set of debt indicators. Results are calculated by this tool; fresh Excel verification remains pending. We are developing this tool iteratively and welcome stakeholder feedback and co-design. Give feedback (please leave confidential data out).

What can I use this for? Start with your completed DSA workbook, change assumptions using its Customized Scenario inputs, and save alternatives to compare and explain.

What does it cover? The supported macroeconomic adjustments and external financing terms. It does not estimate investment benefits or produce a complete official DSA or risk rating.

What do PV, PPG and percentage points mean?

PV means present value: future debt payments expressed in today’s value using the framework discounting assumptions. PPG means public and publicly guaranteed debt. pp means percentage points: 30.00% to 29.84% is a fall of 0.16 percentage points. The ratio’s denominator is shown beside each indicator.

What is different about the two chart styles?

The standard view uses familiar debt-indicator panels, levels and thresholds. The policy view highlights differences from your selected comparison case. Their numbers, years and assumptions are identical. A smaller or larger indicator alone is not a complete policy judgment.

Does a lower debt ratio mean the plan is better?

A lower ratio under one set of assumptions is a comparison result, not a verdict on the whole plan. Review the other indicators, financing costs and availability, and the size and timing of assumed benefits. This view reports selected years, so it cannot identify the annual peak or first threshold crossing. Compare a smaller or delayed benefit as well as the central case.

    How are these summaries calculated?

    Every selected alternative is listed for the indicator chosen above. Each line gives its latest comparable reported observation and its smallest and largest differences across reported years. If differences change sign, the summary says so. Missing observations are identified; these ranges do not establish annual peaks or threshold crossings. Magnitudes of 10²¹ or more use scientific notation. Exactly equal values show 0.00 pp; nonzero differences below 0.005 pp show ≈0.00. The numerical comparison table in step 4 has every value.

    How this tool works

    This tool helps you explore how alternative macroeconomic and financing assumptions affect the supported debt indicators. It uses the workbook’s Customized Scenario mechanism, keeps alternatives separate and provides two views of the same calculated results: familiar LIC-DSF charts and a policy briefing view.

    Start from the assumptions in your workbook

    A DSA baseline can already include planned policies and investment effects where those assumptions are realistic and documented. Check what is included before adding an alternative, so you do not count the same effect twice. The reference baseline and the workbook’s existing customized scenario are separate. Starting a new scenario clears its customized adjustments; it does not redefine the reference baseline.

    Understand the inputs

    The 13 macroeconomic inputs follow the template’s Customized Scenario tabs. Enter adjustments in the displayed units, for one year or a period. Growth changes are percentage points; fiscal and external-flow ratios use percentage points of GDP; public-sector assets use local currency at the workbook’s scale. Financing controls change the supported interest rate, grace period and maturity. Leaving financing unchanged retains the supplied values and formulas.

    An illustrative investment scenario supplies a spending path and an assumed later growth benefit. The tool calculates the debt implications conditional on those inputs. It does not estimate the benefit from the spending. Review timing, implementation capacity, financing availability and the consistency of linked fiscal and external assumptions. A smaller or delayed benefit is a useful sensitivity case; an assumed benefit is not an estimated return.

    Does this calculation use AI?

    No. This is a local, deterministic calculation tool. Supported formula results are repeatable with unchanged workbook inputs and engine version. Date-dependent formulas introduced into a different workbook can change with the date. It evaluates supported workbook formulas; it does not ask an AI model, randomly sample outcomes or estimate policy benefits. You supply and explain the assumptions. Installation needs the documented dependencies; calculations then run locally.

    Read results and their limits

    This version reports six indicators at selected projection years: the first year and years 3, 6, 11, 16 and 21. It does not yet show every annual observation or the complete official stress-test suite. Do not infer a peak or first threshold crossing between the displayed years. Both chart views retain the same numbers and evidence.

    Workbook formulas are evaluated by this tool’s Python calculation engine. Macros are not run and external links are not refreshed. Agreement with saved workbook values is useful evidence, but it is not a fresh Microsoft Excel comparison or an official institutional judgment.

    How does this fit the LIC-DSF guidance?

    This app explores and explains alternative assumptions. The guidance distinguishes illustrative policy and financing scenarios from customized stress tests used in formal risk analysis. This version does not run the full stress-test suite or assign a risk rating. Its results do not constitute IMF or World Bank endorsement.

    The 2024 references below discuss climate-related analysis. Their requirements to explain assumptions, avoid double counting and test financing realism also inform this general tool; that broader application is our interpretation, not an endorsement.     Connection to longer-term policy analysis

    The proposed revised LIC-DSF includes a complementary long-term module for exploring policy and financing alternatives with inputs from other models. This application currently supports the existing template’s Customized Scenario mechanism. It is not an implementation of that proposed module or an automated connection to an upstream model.

    Read the 2024 IMF/WB guidance supplement (paragraphs 4–5) and the 2026 review background note (paragraphs 26–34) for the institutional context.
