# From assumptions to a briefing

Start with a question: what changes if growth is a little lower, financing costs more, or an investment programme costs money now and provides benefits later?

This tool lets you explore those assumptions through the customized-scenario mechanism in a supported LIC-DSF workbook. It keeps separate cases and your explanations, then produces charts and data you can share. It does not estimate a programme's returns or decide which assumptions are credible.

**This is an early-stage preview.** We welcome iterative co-design with ministries of finance, international financial institutions and other stakeholders. Calculations are deterministic and local, without AI inference. Fresh Microsoft Excel verification remains pending.

## Make your first comparison

1. [Download and start](installation.md) the Windows or Mac application.
2. Load the [official illustrative workbook](template-source.md). Its Ghana-labelled figures are an example, not an official country forecast or DSA.
3. Follow the [two-case walkthrough](tutorial.md): review the baseline, save a no-change control and a lower-growth case, and explain the change.
4. Calculate both cases and compare them using the familiar LIC-DSF charts and policy briefing charts together.
5. Review and download the complete briefing packet. It contains both PDF reports, charts, underlying tables, assumptions, explanations and evidence.

You enter adjustments for each projection year. The preview reports debt indicators at six selected years; lines between those observations are not independently calculated annual results. [Methods](methodology.md) explains this and other boundaries.

## Understand what you are sharing

A briefing is for communicating results. A [scenario file](exchange-recovery.md) is for reusing one case's inputs with the same exact workbook. A private workspace backup is for recovering all your work. They serve different purposes and contain different information.

Review intentionally shared labels, assumptions and explanations before sending anything. The app excludes private journals and the source workbook from briefing packets, but it cannot know whether text you deliberately share is confidential.
